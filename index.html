Puzzles built around the structure of Chinese

This is not about memorising characters or guessing answers: you choose based on stroke order, components, character sequence and meaning. Every mode keeps a clear goal, immediate feedback and progress you can pick up again.

笔

Stroke-by-stroke tracing

The first three levels guide you clearly; from level 4 all strokes are shown below — tap them in the right order and only the correct one is traced.

成

Drag-and-drop idioms

Drag each tile into its target slot, then tap to read the meaning; unfinished tiles can be double-tapped back to the parts tray.

文

Classical text ordering

Find the characters in the original order from a shuffled pile and pick up where you left off; a reshuffle button rearranges the parts tray at any time.

适

Multiple languages and sizes

Simplified Chinese and English are supported. On iPad, portrait reads in a single column like a phone, while landscape lays out the text, the parts and the puzzle board side by side.

Three modes, three rhythms

Characters train stroke order, idioms train word order and meaning, and classical texts rebuild long passages. Level counts and goals stay clear, so short repeated sessions work as well as longer study.

Standard stroke hints keep getting refined

Hints for easily confused strokes — such as 横撇, 竖折, 竖弯钩, 卧钩, 点 and 捺 — are now aligned with the stroke-order data.

Gameplay

Choose your own strokes from level 4

The next stroke is no longer revealed. All strokes are laid out below — just tap the right one, no dragging needed.

Layout

iPad portrait/landscape and interface copy

The main menu, idioms, classical texts and tracing screens all adapt to orientation, and level titles plus puzzle progress are localised for the supported languages.

Designed for mobile learning and casual puzzling

The flow favours quick starts, clear goals and immediate feedback. Finishing a level unlocks the next one, and classical texts can be resumed — good for commutes, after-school practice or playing together with family.
